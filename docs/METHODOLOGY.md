# Methodology — from field observation to verified outcome

## Investigative case lifecycle

1. **Reported** — a clearly labeled report, not an established defect. No ranking of allegations without corroboration.
2. **Investigating** — collect road segment + direction + time window, traffic volume, observed/free-flow travel times, source, method, dates and coverage. Verify jurisdiction and identify alternatives.
3. **Proposed** — compare at least two alternatives, including do-nothing, with safety, accessibility, transit, equity, cost and downstream network effects.
4. **In progress** — record implementation owner, decision, dates, official references and practical follow-up.
5. **Evaluating** — pre-register before/after outcome metrics and a comparison strategy; distinguish concurrent changes and demand effects.
6. **Resolved** — only after a documented outcome, with caveats. An ignored request is not a resolution.

Statuses indicate *case workflow*, **not** a verified fact about a public agency.

## Evidence levels

- **synthetic:** invented examples for demonstrations. Never count as verified reports or findings.
- **unverified:** a lead, eyewitness account or incomplete document. Label explicitly as such.
- **documented:** referenced primary sources with dates and provenance; still not automatically proof of causation.

Evidence must be evaluated by data coverage, collection methodology, geographic/temporal alignment, independence, reproducibility, uncertainty and conflicts of interest.

## Congestion screening

`vehicle-minutes excess = max(0, average observed route time − matched free-flow route time) × matched vehicle flow`.

Assumptions for a meaningful number:
- Same route segment, direction, travel-time definition and window for all inputs.
- Clearly defined free-flow baseline, representative sampling and expected measurement error.
- No multiplication of a snapshot travel time by an unrelated annual count.
- Separate reporting of day type, seasonality, transit/bus priority, pedestrians, cyclists and safety.
- No conflation of vehicle-minute and **person-minute** delay; vehicle occupancy is generally unknown.
- Cases with missing, heterogeneous or unverified measurements remain **unranked** until valid matching data exist.

First interventions should generally examine signal settings, blocked junctions, queue spillback, curb/parking management, transit priority and operational constraints **before** expensive capacity projects; each candidate requires local engineering and safety review.

## Before-and-after experiment

Define the baseline, outcome measures, sampling windows, control/comparison location, anticipated externalities and minimum observation period before implementing a change. Look for displaced traffic, induced demand and worsening of transit or vulnerable road-user outcomes. Report confidence bounds when possible; use observational language when causal identification is weak.

## Accountability and publication

Record precisely who is responsible for *what*, based on an official jurisdiction source; don't presume an agency is negligent. Keep a dated log of contact, requested action, response and follow-up. Publish both verified responses and unresolved questions. Allow factual corrections and remove personal information from submissions.

## Privacy and safety

Never collect individual route traces, phone identifiers, car registration plates, sensitive personal location or unauthorized images. Field researchers should observe from public, lawful, safe locations and should not obstruct traffic. Redact incidental personal information. Protect minors and private individuals. This repository is **not** a hotline for urgent road hazards; report emergencies through the proper public emergency channels.
